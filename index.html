PPHN

Pressure-Related
  • Flow: R-to-L shunt via PDA
  • Pressure: PA > Aortic pressure
  • Saturation: Upper body pink, lower body blue
  • Mechanism: Elevated pulmonary vascular resistance

Critical Coarctation

Anatomical Obstruction
  • Flow: R-to-L shunt via DA
  • Pressure: Low pressure distal to coarctation
  • Saturation: Upper body pink, lower body blue
  • Mechanism: Anatomical narrowing of aorta

🔍 Key Clinical Distinction

Pre-ductal vs Post-ductal Oxygen Saturation:
Measure SpO2 in the right hand (pre-ductal) and either foot (post-ductal).
A difference of >3-5% suggests differential cyanosis from either PPHN or ductal-dependent systemic circulation.

Clinical Pearls & Diagnostics

📊 Diagnostic Approach

• Pre/post-ductal SpO₂ difference >5%
• Echocardiography to confirm anatomy
• 4-limb blood pressures
• Hyperoxia test (limited value in differential cyanosis)

⚠️ PPHN Management

• Optimize ventilation and oxygenation
• Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO)
• Maintain adequate blood pressure
• Treat underlying cause (MAS, sepsis, etc.)

🚨 Critical Coarctation

• Prostaglandin E₁ to maintain ductal patency
• Immediate cardiology consultation
• Monitor for metabolic acidosis
• Surgical repair required

🔬 Pathophysiology Recap

• Normal: L→R PDA, well-oxygenated
• PPHN: High PVR, R→L PDA, pressure-related
• Coarctation: Anatomical obstruction, R→L DA
• Both show differential cyanosis
